## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> Trust behaves strangely. For a long time, it seems to grow very little. A customer buys once, then waits. A new neighbor nods politely but keeps a distance. Then, almost without warning, trust arrives all at once. The customer begins recommending you to everyone. The neighbor hands you a spare key. Trust, in other words, is slow, then sudden.
>
> Why does it work this way? One reason is that trust is not so much a feeling as a record. Every time you keep a promise, you add a small entry to that record. The more entries there are, the more confident other people become. Early on, a single good experience counts for little, because people know that one success could be luck. But after ten or twenty consistent experiences, the record becomes too strong to ignore.
>
> This explains why early customers are often the hardest to win. A new business has no record at all. People would rather pay a little more at a familiar shop than take a risk on an unknown one. In this situation, a lower price is often less effective than a clear guarantee. Customers care less about saving money than about avoiding disappointment.
>
> The same pattern also works in reverse, and this is the uncomfortable part. Trust takes years to build, but it can be lost in a single afternoon. Losing trust is far easier than earning it. A single broken promise can undo the effect of ten kept ones. Studies of customer behavior suggest that people tend to remember bad experiences more vividly than good ones. If that is true, a business must protect its record at least as carefully as it builds it.
>
> What does this mean for someone starting out? First, consistency matters more than brilliance. A café that serves good coffee every day is better than one that serves amazing coffee only on some days. Second, small promises are no less important than big ones. Answering a message on time or delivering exactly what was described may seem minor, but these are the entries that slowly fill the record. Third, patience is required. The longer you keep your word, the sooner the "sudden" moment seems to arrive.
>
> Trust, then, is one of the most valuable things a business can own, and one of the slowest to earn. It cannot be bought with advertising, and it cannot be rushed. It can only be built, one kept promise at a time, until one day, more and more people decide that you are worth believing.

어휘: consistency 한결같음 · brilliance 탁월함

**㉘ A café that serves good coffee every day is better than one that serves amazing coffee only on some days.**
매일 좋은 커피를 내는 카페가 어떤 날만 놀라운 커피를 내는 카페보다 낫다.
어휘: one (= a café) 대용

**㉙ Second, small promises are no less important than big ones.**
둘째, 작은 약속은 큰 약속 못지않게 중요하다.
어휘: no less ~ than … …못지않게 ~한

**㉚ Answering a message on time or delivering exactly what was described may seem minor, but these are the entries that slowly fill the record.**
메시지에 제때 답하고 설명한 그대로 물건을 보내는 일은 사소해 보이지만, 바로 이런 것들이 기록을 천천히 채운다.
어휘: on time 제때 · minor 사소한

**㉛ Third, patience is required.**
셋째, 인내가 필요하다.
어휘: patience 인내

**㉜ The longer you keep your word, the sooner the "sudden" moment seems to arrive.**
약속을 오래 지킬수록 그 "갑작스러운" 순간은 더 빨리 오는 것 같다.
어휘: keep one's word 약속을 지키다

### 문단 6

**㉝ Trust, then, is one of the most valuable things a business can own, and one of the slowest to earn.**
그러니까 신뢰는 가게가 가질 수 있는 가장 값진 것 중 하나이면서, 얻는 데 가장 오래 걸리는 것 중 하나다.
어휘: one of the + 최상급 + 복수명사

**㉞ It cannot be bought with advertising, and it cannot be rushed.**
광고로 살 수도 없고, 서둘러 얻을 수도 없다.
어휘: advertising 광고 · rush 서두르다

**㉟ It can only be built, one kept promise at a time, until one day, more and more people decide that you are worth believing.**
신뢰는 오직 약속을 하나씩 지켜 가며 쌓을 수밖에 없다. 그러다 어느 날, 점점 더 많은 사람들이 당신을 믿어도 된다고 판단하게 된다.
어휘: one at a time 한 번에 하나씩 · 비교급 and 비교급 점점 더 ~한

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 비교 구문 심화

10편에서 비교급·최상급의 형태를 배웠다면, 이번 편은 비교 구문이 만들어 내는 **의미의 뉘앙스**를 다룹니다.

| 구문 | 의미 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| the 비교급 ~, the 비교급 … | ~할수록 더 …하다 | **The more** entries there are, **the more** confident other people become. |
| not so much A as B | A라기보다는 B | Trust is **not so much** a feeling **as** a record. |
| would rather A than B | B하느니 차라리 A하겠다 | People **would rather** pay a little more **than** take a risk. |
| less ~ than | ~보다 덜 …한 | a lower price is often **less effective than** a clear guarantee |
| care less about A than about B | B보다 A에 덜 신경 쓰다 | Customers **care less about** saving money **than about** avoiding disappointment. |
| no less ~ than | ~ 못지않게 …한 | Small promises are **no less important than** big ones. |
| as + 부사 + as | ~만큼 …하게 | protect its record at least **as carefully as** it builds it |
| 비교급 and 비교급 | 점점 더 ~한 | **more and more** people |
| one of the + 최상급 + 복수명사 | 가장 ~한 것 중 하나 | **one of the most valuable things** |

### 2. 비교급 강조

| 강조어 | 예 | 의미 |
|---|---|---|
| far, much, even, still | **far** easier than earning it | 훨씬 더 쉬운 |
| a little, slightly | pay **a little** more | 조금 더 |

very는 비교급을 강조하지 못합니다. very easier (❌), far easier (✅).

### 3. 비교 대상의 병렬

비교되는 두 대상은 같은 형태여야 합니다(29편 병렬 구조 예고).

- ✅ **Losing** trust is far easier than **earning** it. (동명사 vs 동명사)
- ✅ care less **about** saving money than **about** avoiding disappointment (전치사구 vs 전치사구)
- ❌ Losing trust is far easier than to earn it.

### 4. 대용어 one / ones

> A café that serves good coffee every day is better than **one** that serves amazing coffee…
> A single broken promise can undo the effect of ten kept **ones**.

비교 구문에서는 같은 명사를 반복하지 않으려고 one(단수)·ones(복수)를 자주 씁니다. 28편에서 대용과 생략을 본격적으로 다룹니다.
