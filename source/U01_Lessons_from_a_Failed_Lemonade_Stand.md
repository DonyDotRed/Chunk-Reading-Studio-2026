## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> At the age of fifteen, I launched what I would now describe as my first commercial venture: a roadside lemonade stand. Over the course of a single week, the stand generated nine dollars in revenue against forty dollars in expenditure, a net loss of thirty-one dollars. At the time, I interpreted this outcome as a personal failure and sought to forget it as quickly as possible. A decade later, however, the episode appears less as an embarrassment than as a compact illustration of three errors that are common among first-time entrepreneurs. This essay revisits the venture retrospectively in order to identify those errors and to consider what they reveal about the early stages of building a business.
>
> The first and most consequential error concerned the timing of investment. Before a single unit had been sold, I had committed nearly all of my available capital to ingredients, equipment, and signage. In effect, I treated the venture as though demand had already been established, when in fact it remained entirely hypothetical. This sequence, in which resources are allocated before the core assumption is tested, inverts the order that many practitioners now recommend. A more prudent approach would have involved selling a small batch with minimal equipment and expanding only once there was evidence that customers existed. The cost of such a test would have been a few dollars; the cost of omitting it was the greater part of my savings.
>
> The second error related to location. I positioned the stand on a quiet residential street, largely because it was convenient for me rather than accessible to potential customers. Over an entire morning, only three vehicles passed, and none stopped. The underlying problem was not the quality of the product but the absence of a market within reach of it. In more formal terms, I had failed to consider distribution: the question of how a product actually reaches the people who might want it. Even an excellent product cannot generate revenue if no one encounters it.
>
> The third error, and arguably the most instructive, was the absence of any attempt to gather information from potential customers. I did not ask neighbors whether they wanted lemonade, at what price, or at what time of day. Consequently, every decision I made, from the size of the cups to the design of the sign, rested on my own assumptions. Had I conducted even a handful of brief conversations, I might have discovered that afternoon foot traffic near the local park was considerably higher, or that a lower price would have attracted more buyers. The lack of feedback did not merely produce poor decisions; it prevented me from recognizing that my decisions were poor until the losses had already accumulated.
>
> It would be misleading, however, to suggest that the venture produced nothing of value. Although the financial outcome was negative, the experience generated knowledge that has since proved transferable to considerably larger projects. Each of the three errors corresponds to a principle I now apply routinely: test demand before investing heavily, go where potential customers already are, and ask before assuming. These principles are neither original nor sophisticated, yet they are frequently neglected, perhaps precisely because they appear obvious in retrospect.
>
> In conclusion, the lemonade stand failed not because the underlying idea was inherently flawed but because the venture was executed in an order that maximized risk and minimized learning. The thirty-one dollars I lost may reasonably be regarded as the tuition for an early and unusually concrete lesson in entrepreneurship. Viewed in this light, the failure was not the end of a career in business but, in a modest sense, its beginning.

### 문단 1

**① At the age of fifteen, I launched what I would now describe as my first commercial venture: a roadside lemonade stand.**

어휘: launch 개시하다, 출시하다 · commercial 상업적인 · venture 모험적 사업 · roadside 길가의

**② Over the course of a single week, the stand generated nine dollars in revenue against forty dollars in expenditure, a net loss of thirty-one dollars.**
일주일 동안 가판대는 지출 40달러에 수입 9달러를 올려, 31달러의 순손실을 냈다.
어휘: over the course of ~하는 동안 · generate 창출하다 · revenue 수입, 매출 · against ~에 대비하여 · expenditure 지출 · net loss 순손실

**③ At the time, I interpreted this outcome as a personal failure and sought to forget it as quickly as possible.**
당시 나는 이 결과를 개인적 실패로 받아들였고, 최대한 빨리 잊으려 했다.
어휘: interpret 해석하다 · outcome 결과 · seek–sought to ~하려고 애쓰다

**④ A decade later, however, the episode appears less as an embarrassment than as a compact illustration of three errors that are common among first-time entrepreneurs.**
그러나 10년이 지난 지금, 그 일은 창피한 기억이라기보다 처음 창업하는 사람들이 흔히 저지르는 세 가지 오류를 압축해 보여 주는 사례로 보인다.
어휘: decade 10년 · episode 일화 · less A than B A라기보다 B · compact 압축된 · illustration 예시 · entrepreneur 창업가

**⑤ This essay revisits the venture retrospectively in order to identify those errors and to consider what they reveal about the early stages of building a business.**
이 글은 그 오류들을 확인하고, 그것들이 사업 초기 단계에 대해 무엇을 말해 주는지 고찰하기 위해 그 일을 되돌아본다.
어휘: revisit 재검토하다 · retrospectively 회고적으로 · identify 확인하다 · consider 고찰하다 · reveal 드러내다

### 문단 2

**⑥ The first and most consequential error concerned the timing of investment.**
첫 번째이자 가장 중대한 오류는 투자 시기와 관련된 것이었다.
어휘: consequential 중대한 · concern ~와 관련되다 · investment 투자

**⑦ Before a single unit had been sold, I had committed nearly all of my available capital to ingredients, equipment, and signage.**
한 잔도 팔기 전에, 나는 쓸 수 있는 자본 거의 전부를 재료와 장비, 간판에 쏟아부었다.
어휘: unit (판매) 단위 · commit (자원을) 투입하다 · available 이용 가능한 · capital 자본 · equipment 장비 · signage 간판류

**⑧ In effect, I treated the venture as though demand had already been established, when in fact it remained entirely hypothetical.**
사실상 나는 수요가 이미 확인된 것처럼 행동했지만, 실제로 수요는 전적으로 가설에 불과했다.
어휘: in effect 사실상 · as though 마치 ~처럼 · demand 수요 · establish 확립하다 · when (양보) ~인데도 · remain 여전히 ~이다 · hypothetical 가설적인

**⑨ This sequence, in which resources are allocated before the core assumption is tested, inverts the order that many practitioners now recommend.**
핵심 가정을 검증하기도 전에 자원부터 배분하는 이런 순서는, 오늘날 많은 실무자들이 권하는 순서를 거꾸로 뒤집은 것이다.
어휘: sequence 순서 · resource 자원 · allocate 배분하다 · core 핵심의 · assumption 가정 · invert 뒤집다 · practitioner 실무자

**⑩ A more prudent approach would have involved selling a small batch with minimal equipment and expanding only once there was evidence that customers existed.**
더 신중한 방법은 최소한의 장비로 소량을 팔아 보고, 손님이 있다는 증거가 생긴 뒤에야 규모를 늘리는 것이었을 터이다.
어휘: prudent 신중한 · involve 포함하다 · batch 1회분 · minimal 최소한의 · expand 확장하다 · evidence 증거 · exist 존재하다

**⑪ The cost of such a test would have been a few dollars; the cost of omitting it was the greater part of my savings.**
그런 시험에는 몇 달러면 충분했겠지만, 그것을 건너뛴 대가는 내 저금의 대부분이었다.
어휘: omit 생략하다 · the greater part of ~의 대부분

### 문단 3

**⑫ The second error related to location.**
두 번째 오류는 위치에 관한 것이었다.
어휘: relate to ~와 관련되다 · location 위치

**⑬ I positioned the stand on a quiet residential street, largely because it was convenient for me rather than accessible to potential customers.**
나는 조용한 주택가에 가판대를 놓았는데, 잠재 고객이 오기 쉬워서가 아니라 주로 내가 편해서였다.
어휘: position 배치하다 · residential 주거의 · largely 주로 · convenient 편리한 · accessible 접근 가능한 · potential 잠재적인

**⑭ Over an entire morning, only three vehicles passed, and none stopped.**
오전 내내 차는 세 대만 지나갔고, 한 대도 서지 않았다.
어휘: entire 전체의 · vehicle 차량

**⑮ The underlying problem was not the quality of the product but the absence of a market within reach of it.**
근본적인 문제는 상품의 품질이 아니라, 상품이 닿을 수 있는 범위에 시장이 없다는 것이었다.
어휘: underlying 근본적인 · quality 품질 · absence 부재 · within reach of ~이 닿는 곳에

**⑯ In more formal terms, I had failed to consider distribution: the question of how a product actually reaches the people who might want it.**
좀 더 전문적으로 말하면, 나는 유통, 즉 상품이 그것을 원할 수도 있는 사람들에게 실제로 어떻게 닿는가 하는 문제를 고려하지 못했다.
어휘: in ~ terms ~의 관점에서 · distribution 유통

**⑰ Even an excellent product cannot generate revenue if no one encounters it.**
아무리 훌륭한 상품도 아무도 접하지 못하면 수익을 낼 수 없다.
어휘: encounter 접하다

### 문단 4

**⑱ The third error, and arguably the most instructive, was the absence of any attempt to gather information from potential customers.**
세 번째이자 가장 교훈적인 오류는, 잠재 고객에게서 정보를 모으려는 시도가 전혀 없었다는 점이다.
어휘: arguably 거의 틀림없이 · instructive 교훈적인 · attempt 시도 · gather 모으다

**⑲ I did not ask neighbors whether they wanted lemonade, at what price, or at what time of day.**
나는 이웃들에게 레모네이드를 원하는지, 얼마면 사겠는지, 언제 마시고 싶은지 묻지 않았다.

**⑳ Consequently, every decision I made, from the size of the cups to the design of the sign, rested on my own assumptions.**
그 결과 컵 크기부터 간판 디자인까지 내가 내린 모든 결정은 내 짐작에만 기대고 있었다.
어휘: consequently 결과적으로 · from A to B A에서 B까지 · rest on ~에 근거하다

**㉑ Had I conducted even a handful of brief conversations, I might have discovered that afternoon foot traffic near the local park was considerably higher, or that a lower price would have attracted more buyers.**
짧은 대화를 몇 번만 나눴더라도, 오후에 동네 공원 근처 유동 인구가 훨씬 많다거나, 값을 낮췄으면 사는 사람이 더 많았으리라는 걸 알았을지도 모른다.
어휘: Had I p.p. (if 생략 도치) · conduct 수행하다 · a handful of 소수의 · brief 짧은 · foot traffic 유동 인구 · considerably 상당히 · attract 끌어들이다

**㉒ The lack of feedback did not merely produce poor decisions; it prevented me from recognizing that my decisions were poor until the losses had already accumulated.**
피드백이 없어서 잘못된 결정을 내렸을 뿐 아니라, 손실이 이미 쌓일 때까지 그 결정이 잘못됐다는 사실조차 알아차리지 못했다.
어휘: lack 부족 · merely 단지 · prevent A from -ing A가 ~하는 것을 막다 · recognize 인식하다 · loss 손실 · accumulate 쌓이다

### 문단 5

**㉓ It would be misleading, however, to suggest that the venture produced nothing of value.**
그러나 그 일이 아무 가치도 남기지 않았다고 말한다면 오해일 것이다.
어휘: misleading 오해를 부르는 · suggest 암시하다 · of value 가치 있는

**㉔ Although the financial outcome was negative, the experience generated knowledge that has since proved transferable to considerably larger projects.**
재정적 결과는 마이너스였지만, 그 경험에서 얻은 지식은 이후 훨씬 큰 프로젝트에도 적용할 수 있는 것으로 드러났다.
어휘: financial 재정적인 · prove ~임이 드러나다 · transferable 다른 곳에 적용 가능한

**㉕ Each of the three errors corresponds to a principle I now apply routinely: test demand before investing heavily, go where potential customers already are, and ask before assuming.**
세 가지 오류는 각각 내가 지금 늘 적용하는 원칙과 짝을 이룬다. 크게 투자하기 전에 수요를 검증하라, 잠재 고객이 이미 있는 곳으로 가라, 짐작하기 전에 물어라.
어휘: correspond to ~에 대응하다 · principle 원칙 · routinely 일상적으로 · assume 가정하다

**㉖ These principles are neither original nor sophisticated, yet they are frequently neglected, perhaps precisely because they appear obvious in retrospect.**
이 원칙들은 독창적이지도 정교하지도 않지만 자주 무시되는데, 어쩌면 돌이켜 보면 너무 뻔해 보이기 때문일 것이다.
어휘: neither A nor B A도 B도 아닌 · original 독창적인 · sophisticated 정교한 · neglect 간과하다 · precisely 바로 · obvious 뻔한 · in retrospect 돌이켜 보면

### 문단 6

**㉗ In conclusion, the lemonade stand failed not because the underlying idea was inherently flawed but because the venture was executed in an order that maximized risk and minimized learning.**
결론적으로 레모네이드 가판대가 실패한 것은 아이디어 자체에 결함이 있어서가 아니라, 위험은 최대로 키우고 배움은 최소로 줄이는 순서로 실행했기 때문이다.
어휘: not because A but because B · inherently 본질적으로 · flawed 결함 있는 · execute 실행하다 · maximize 최대화하다 · minimize 최소화하다

**㉘ The thirty-one dollars I lost may reasonably be regarded as the tuition for an early and unusually concrete lesson in entrepreneurship.**
내가 잃은 31달러는 창업에 관한 이르고도 유난히 구체적인 수업의 수업료였다고 봐도 무리가 없다.
어휘: regard A as B A를 B로 여기다 · tuition 수업료 · concrete 구체적인

**㉙ Viewed in this light, the failure was not the end of a career in business but, in a modest sense, its beginning.**
이렇게 보면 그 실패는 사업가로서의 끝이 아니라, 소박한 의미에서 그 시작이었다.
어휘: in this light 이런 관점에서 · Viewed (분사구문) · modest 소박한

## 학술 영어 포인트

### 1. 같은 경험, 다른 문체: 01편과 U01 비교

01편과 U01은 같은 사건을 다룹니다. 차이는 정보가 아니라 **문체(register)**에 있습니다.

| 01편 (B1 서사) | U01 (C1 분석) | 변화 |
|---|---|---|
| I decided to start my first business. | I launched what I would now describe as my first commercial venture. | 일상 동사 → 학술 동사, 현재 시점의 재해석 |
| I had spent forty dollars, but I had earned only nine. | the stand generated nine dollars in revenue against forty dollars in expenditure, a net loss of thirty-one dollars | 사람 주어 → 사물 주어, 경제 용어, 순손실 명시 |
| I spent almost all of my savings before I sold a single cup. | Before a single unit had been sold, I had committed nearly all of my available capital… | 수동태, 학술 어휘 (unit, commit, capital) |
| I chose a place where there were no customers. | I had failed to consider distribution: the question of how a product actually reaches the people who might want it. | 구체적 사건 → 추상 개념 + 정의 |
| I never asked people what they actually wanted. | The third error… was the absence of any attempt to gather information from potential customers. | 동사 중심 → 명사화 (absence, attempt) |

### 2. 회고적 분석 에세이의 구조

| 문단 | 기능 | 핵심 표현 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 사건 요약 + 재해석 + 목적 진술 | **This essay revisits… in order to identify… and to consider…** |
| 2–4 | 오류 1·2·3 분석 | **The first and most consequential error concerned…** / **The second error related to…** / **The third error, and arguably the most instructive, …** |
| 5 | 양보적 전환 (부정적 결과에도 가치가 있음) | **It would be misleading, however, to suggest that…** |
| 6 | 결론 + 재해석의 최종 진술 | **In conclusion, … not because… but because…** / **Viewed in this light, …** |

**서열화 표현에 주목하세요.** 오류를 단순히 first, second, third로 나열하지 않고 most consequential(가장 중대한), arguably the most instructive(아마도 가장 교훈적인)처럼 **평가를 덧붙여** 서열을 매깁니다. 분석적 글쓰기의 특징입니다.

### 3. 이번 편의 학술 어휘

| 영역 | 어휘 |
|---|---|
| 경제·경영 | venture, revenue, expenditure, net loss, capital, allocate, demand, distribution |
| 분석·평가 | consequential, hypothetical, underlying, inherently, flawed, instructive, misleading |
| 논리 연결 | in effect, consequently, in retrospect, in this light, correspond to, rest on |
| 행동 동사 | launch, commit, establish, invert, execute, maximize, minimize, accumulate |

### 4. 고급 구문 복습

| 구문 | 이 글의 예 | 기초 편 |
|---|---|---|
| less A than B | appears **less** as an embarrassment **than** as a compact illustration | 27편 not so much A as B |
| if 생략 도치 | **Had I conducted** even a handful of brief conversations, … | 21편 |
| 가주어 + 양보적 전환 | **It would be misleading**, however, **to suggest that**… | 20편·24편 |
