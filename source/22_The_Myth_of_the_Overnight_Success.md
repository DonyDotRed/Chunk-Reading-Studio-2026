## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> Every few months, a new story appears online about a young person who became successful "overnight." A teenager's video suddenly gets ten million views. A small app jumps to the top of the download chart. A simple product sells out in a single day. These stories are exciting, and they spread quickly. However, they almost always leave out the most important part.
>
> It is not the final moment that makes someone successful; it is the hundreds of quiet days before it. The video that went viral was usually the creator's two-hundredth video, not the first. The app that topped the chart had often failed in three earlier versions. What the public sees is a single bright moment. What it does not see is the long, boring road that led there.
>
> Rarely do these stories mention the failures. Never do they describe the evenings spent fixing small problems while friends were relaxing. Only after someone succeeds do we become interested in their story, and by then, we hear only the happy ending. Little do most readers realize that the "overnight" success took five or ten years.
>
> Why does this myth matter? It matters because it changes how young people judge their own progress. If you believe that success should arrive quickly, you will feel like a failure after your first few attempts. You do try, but nothing seems to happen, so you quit. Not only does the myth make people impatient, but it also makes them ashamed of normal, healthy struggle.
>
> The truth is less exciting but far more useful. So common is slow progress that it should be considered the rule, not the exception. Most people who build something lasting do so through small, repeated actions that look unimpressive day by day. It is the habit of showing up, not a sudden flash of luck, that separates those who succeed from those who give up.
>
> The next time you read about an overnight success, try asking a different question. Do not ask, "How did they do it so fast?" Ask, "What were they doing for the five years before anyone noticed?" The answer will almost always be the same: they kept going when nobody was watching. And that, unlike luck, is something every one of us can choose.

**㉕ The next time you read about an overnight success, try asking a different question.**

어휘: try -ing 시험 삼아 ~해 보다

**㉖ Do not ask, "How did they do it so fast?"**
"어떻게 그렇게 빨리 해냈을까?"라고 묻지 말자.

**㉗ Ask, "What were they doing for the five years before anyone noticed?"**
"누군가 알아채기 전 5년 동안 그들은 무엇을 하고 있었을까?"라고 물어보자.

**㉘ The answer will almost always be the same: they kept going when nobody was watching.**
답은 거의 언제나 같을 것이다. 아무도 보지 않을 때도 그들은 계속 나아갔다는 것.
어휘: keep going 계속하다

**㉙ And that, unlike luck, is something every one of us can choose.**
그리고 그것은 운과 달리, 우리 누구나 선택할 수 있는 것이다.

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 강조 구문

| 형태 | 구조 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| It is ~ that … | 강조할 말을 It is와 that 사이에 둠 | **It is** not the final moment **that** makes someone successful. |
| It is A, not B, that … | 대조 강조 | **It is** the habit of showing up, **not** a sudden flash of luck, **that** separates… |
| What ~ is … | 의사분열문: "~하는 것은 바로 …이다" | **What** the public sees **is** a single bright moment. |
| do/does/did + 동사원형 | 동사 강조: "정말로, 분명히" | You **do try**, but nothing seems to happen. |

**It is ~ that 구문 확인법.** It is와 that을 지우고 남은 말로 문장이 완성되면 강조 구문입니다.
- ~~It is~~ the habit of showing up ~~that~~ separates those who succeed… → The habit of showing up separates those who succeed… ✅

### 2. 부정어 도치

부정어(never, rarely, little, not only 등)가 문장 맨 앞으로 나오면 **조동사 + 주어**의 의문문 어순이 됩니다.

| 보통 어순 | 도치 어순 |
|---|---|
| These stories **rarely mention** the failures. | **Rarely do** these stories **mention** the failures. |
| They **never describe** the evenings… | **Never do** they **describe** the evenings… |
| Most readers **little realize** that… | **Little do** most readers **realize** that… |
| The myth **not only makes** people impatient, but… | **Not only does** the myth **make** people impatient, but… |
| We become interested **only after** someone succeeds. | **Only after** someone succeeds **do we become** interested. |

**Only after 주의.** "Only after + 절"이 앞에 나오면, 도치는 그 뒤에 오는 **주절**에서 일어납니다. after 절 안은 보통 어순입니다.

### 3. 보어 도치: So + 형용사 + be + 주어

| 보통 어순 | 도치 어순 |
|---|---|
| Slow progress **is so common** that… | **So common is** slow progress that… |

so ~ that(너무 ~해서 …하다) 구문에서 형용사를 강조할 때 씁니다. 격식 있는 글쓰기에 어울립니다.

### 4. 지금까지 나온 도치 정리

| 편 | 도치 유형 | 예 |
|---|---|---|
| 07 | 직접화법 전달동사 | "…," **said the fox**. |
| 21 | if 생략 | **Had I not learned** that lesson… |
| 22 | 부정어·Only·So | **Rarely do** these stories mention… |
| 23 | nor + 조동사 + 주어 | …, **nor should success** be measured only by money. |
