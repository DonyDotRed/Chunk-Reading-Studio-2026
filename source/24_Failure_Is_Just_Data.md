## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> In a science laboratory, an experiment that does not produce the expected result is not usually called a failure. It is called data. Researchers record it, think about why it happened, and design the next experiment. In everyday life, however, we tend to treat unexpected results very differently. When a plan does not work, we often feel embarrassed and may even conclude that we are not talented enough. This essay argues that adopting a more scientific attitude toward failure could help people, and especially young entrepreneurs, learn faster and suffer less.
>
> The scientific method is built around the idea that our first guesses are likely to be wrong. A researcher begins with a hypothesis, a possible explanation that has not yet been proven. She then designs a test that could show the hypothesis to be false. If the result does not support it, she does not abandon science. She adjusts the hypothesis and tries again. Over time, this cycle tends to produce knowledge that is far more reliable than any single guess.
>
> A new business idea can be seen in much the same way. "Students will pay for a textbook-sharing app" is not a fact; it is a hypothesis. Selling to ten classmates is an experiment. If only one buys, the result does not necessarily mean the idea is worthless. It may suggest that the price is too high, the message is unclear, or the wrong group of students was asked. Each of these possibilities could be tested next.
>
> Of course, this comparison has limits. A failed laboratory test rarely costs a scientist her savings, whereas a failed business can cause real financial and emotional harm. It would therefore be unrealistic to claim that failure is always painless. Nevertheless, the scientific attitude seems to reduce the damage in two ways. First, it encourages small, cheap experiments rather than large, risky bets. Second, it separates the result from the person. An experiment can fail without the scientist being a failure.
>
> There is also some evidence that this mindset matters. Research on learning suggests that people who view mistakes as information, rather than as proof of low ability, tend to persist longer when tasks become difficult. While such findings should be interpreted with care, they appear to support the idea that the way we describe failure may affect what we do next.
>
> Perhaps the most useful change is a change in language. Instead of saying, "My idea failed," we might say, "My first test showed that customers did not want this version." The second sentence is longer, but it points toward the next step. In science, a negative result is not the end of the story. It is arguably the most honest part of it.

어휘: suggest 시사하다 (완곡) · unclear 불분명한

**⑱ Each of these possibilities could be tested next.**
이런 가능성 하나하나를 다음 실험에서 확인해 볼 수 있다.
어휘: possibility 가능성

### 문단 4

**⑲ Of course, this comparison has limits.**
물론 이 비교에는 한계가 있다.
어휘: comparison 비교 · limit 한계

**⑳ A failed laboratory test rarely costs a scientist her savings, whereas a failed business can cause real financial and emotional harm.**
실험이 실패했다고 과학자가 저금을 날리는 일은 드물지만, 사업이 실패하면 실제로 금전적·정서적 피해가 생길 수 있다.
어휘: cost + 사람 + 대가 ~에게 …을 잃게 하다 · cause 일으키다 · financial 금전적인 · emotional 정서적인 · harm 피해

**㉑ It would therefore be unrealistic to claim that failure is always painless.**
따라서 실패가 언제나 아프지 않다고 주장하는 것은 비현실적일 것이다.
어휘: it (가주어) · unrealistic 비현실적인 · claim 주장하다 · painless 고통 없는

**㉒ Nevertheless, the scientific attitude seems to reduce the damage in two ways.**
그럼에도 과학적 태도는 두 가지 방식으로 피해를 줄여 주는 것 같다.
어휘: seem to ~인 것 같다 (완곡) · damage 피해

**㉓ First, it encourages small, cheap experiments rather than large, risky bets.**
첫째, 크고 위험한 도박보다 작고 값싼 실험을 하도록 이끈다.
어휘: encourage 장려하다 · bet 내기, 도박

**㉔ Second, it separates the result from the person.**
둘째, 결과와 사람을 분리해 준다.

**㉕ An experiment can fail without the scientist being a failure.**
실험이 실패해도 과학자가 실패자가 되는 것은 아니다.
어휘: without + 의미상 주어 + -ing

### 문단 5

**㉖ There is also some evidence that this mindset matters.**
이런 사고방식이 중요하다는 근거도 어느 정도 있다.
어휘: evidence 증거 · mindset 사고방식

**㉗ Research on learning suggests that people who view mistakes as information, rather than as proof of low ability, tend to persist longer when tasks become difficult.**
학습 연구에 따르면, 실수를 능력이 부족하다는 증거가 아니라 정보로 보는 사람들은 과제가 어려워져도 더 오래 버티는 경향이 있다.
어휘: view A as B A를 B로 보다 · proof 증거 · ability 능력 · persist 끈기 있게 계속하다

**㉘ While such findings should be interpreted with care, they appear to support the idea that the way we describe failure may affect what we do next.**
이런 결과는 신중하게 해석해야 하지만, 실패를 어떻게 표현하느냐가 다음 행동에 영향을 줄 수 있다는 생각을 뒷받침하는 것으로 보인다.
어휘: finding 연구 결과 · interpret 해석하다 · appear to ~인 것으로 보이다 (완곡) · affect 영향을 주다

### 문단 6

**㉙ Perhaps the most useful change is a change in language.**
아마 가장 유용한 변화는 말을 바꾸는 것일 것이다.
어휘: perhaps 아마도 (완곡)

**㉚ Instead of saying, "My idea failed," we might say, "My first test showed that customers did not want this version."**
"내 아이디어는 실패했어"라고 말하는 대신, "첫 실험에서 손님들이 이 버전은 원하지 않는다는 걸 알게 됐어"라고 말해 볼 수 있다.

**㉛ The second sentence is longer, but it points toward the next step.**
두 번째 문장은 더 길지만, 다음 단계를 가리킨다.
어휘: point toward ~쪽을 가리키다

**㉜ In science, a negative result is not the end of the story.**
과학에서 부정적인 결과는 이야기의 끝이 아니다.
어휘: negative 부정적인

**㉝ It is arguably the most honest part of it.**
어쩌면 그 이야기에서 가장 정직한 부분일 것이다.
어휘: arguably 거의 틀림없이 (완곡한 단정)

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 완곡 표현(hedging)이란

학술적인 글에서는 주장의 강도를 **근거의 강도에 맞춥니다**. 증거가 확실하지 않은 주장을 단정하면 신뢰를 잃기 때문입니다. 이렇게 주장의 확신 정도를 조절하는 장치를 hedging이라고 합니다. 대학 수준 영어로 넘어가는 데 가장 중요한 기술 중 하나입니다.

| 단정 (근거보다 강함) | 완곡 (근거에 맞춤) |
|---|---|
| A scientific attitude **helps** people learn faster. | A scientific attitude **could help** people learn faster. |
| People who view mistakes as information **persist** longer. | People who view mistakes as information **tend to persist** longer. |
| The result **means** the idea is worthless. | The result **does not necessarily mean** the idea is worthless. |
| These findings **prove** the idea. | These findings **appear to support** the idea. |

### 2. 완곡 표현의 종류

| 유형 | 표현 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| 조동사 | may, might, could, would | It **may suggest** that the price is too high. / It **would** therefore be unrealistic… |
| 동사 | tend to, seem to, appear to, suggest | we **tend to treat** unexpected results… / they **appear to support** the idea |
| 형용사 구문 | be likely to | our first guesses **are likely to be** wrong |
| 부사 | perhaps, arguably, often, usually | **Perhaps** the most useful change… / It is **arguably** the most honest part. |
| 부분 부정 | not necessarily, not always | **does not necessarily mean** |
| 양 한정 | some, many, in many cases | There is also **some** evidence… |

### 3. 근거를 다루는 학술 표현

| 표현 | 기능 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| Research suggests that ~ | 연구 결과 인용 (단정하지 않음) | **Research on learning suggests that**… |
| should be interpreted with care | 근거의 한계 인정 | such findings **should be interpreted with care** |
| This essay argues that ~ | 글의 주장 명시 | **This essay argues that**… |
| this comparison has limits | 자기 논증의 한계 인정 | **Of course, this comparison has limits.** |

마지막 문단의 "arguably"는 완곡하면서도 꽤 강한 주장을 펼 때 씁니다. "논란의 여지는 있지만 충분히 그렇게 말할 수 있다"는 뜻입니다.

### 4. 미리보기: 의미상 주어 + 동명사

> An experiment can fail **without the scientist being** a failure.

전치사 without 뒤의 동명사 being 앞에 the scientist를 두어, 동명사의 주체를 밝혔습니다. "과학자가 실패자가 되는 일 없이"라는 뜻입니다.
