## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> Talking to your first customer can feel scary. You might worry about saying the wrong thing or being rejected. The good news is that the first conversation does not need to be perfect. Follow these six steps, and you will learn more than you expect.
>
> **Step 1**
>
> Start with people you can reach. Do not look for a famous company or a rich stranger. Think of classmates, neighbors, or members of a club. They are real people with real needs, and they will probably give you honest answers.
>
> **Step 2**
>
> Prepare three questions. Write them on a card before you meet anyone. Good questions are open, not yes-or-no. For example, ask, "What is the hardest part of your morning?" instead of "Do you like my idea?"
>
> **Step 3**
>
> Listen more than you talk. Let the other person speak for most of the conversation. When they stop, wait a second before you reply. People often add the most important detail at the end.
>
> **Step 4**
>
> Don't sell yet. Your goal is to learn, not to win. If you start selling too early, people will only tell you what you want to hear.
>
> **Step 5**
>
> Ask for something small. At the end, ask if you can show them a simple version of your idea next week. A small "yes" now can lead to a bigger "yes" later.
>
> **Step 6**
>
> Write everything down. Right after the conversation, note the words they used and the problems they mentioned. Next, compare your notes after five or six talks. Finally, look for patterns. If three people describe the same problem, you have found something worth solving.
>
> Remember, your first customer is not just a buyer. They are your first teacher.

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 명령문의 형태

| 형태 | 구조 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| 긍정 명령 | 동사원형으로 시작 | **Prepare** three questions. / **Listen** more than you talk. |
| 부정 명령 | Don't / Do not + 동사원형 | **Don't sell** yet. / **Do not look** for a famous company. |
| Let 명령 | Let + O + 동사원형 | **Let** the other person **speak**. |
| 명령문 + and | ~하라, 그러면 … | **Follow** these six steps, **and** you will learn more. |

명령문은 주어(you)를 생략한 문장입니다. 안내문, 매뉴얼, 레시피에서 가장 많이 쓰이며, 영어에서는 무례한 표현이 아니라 명확한 지시 방식입니다. 더 공손하게 하려면 앞에 Please를 붙이거나 Could you…? 형태로 바꿉니다.

**명령문 + or**도 함께 기억하세요. Write it down, **or** you will forget it. (적어 두세요, 그렇지 않으면 잊어버릴 거예요.)

### 2. 순서를 나타내는 연결어

| 연결어 | 의미 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| before | ~하기 전에 | Write them on a card **before** you meet anyone. |
| when | ~할 때 | **When** they stop, wait a second. |
| at the end | 마지막에 | **At the end**, ask if you can show them… |
| right after | ~직후에 | **Right after** the conversation, note the words… |
| next | 다음으로 | **Next**, compare your notes. |
| finally | 마지막으로 | **Finally**, look for patterns. |

### 3. 미리보기: worth -ing

> You have found something **worth solving**.

worth 뒤에는 동명사가 와서 "~할 가치가 있는"이라는 뜻이 됩니다. 01편의 "lessons that were worth much more than nine dollars"처럼 worth 뒤에 명사(금액)가 올 수도 있습니다.
