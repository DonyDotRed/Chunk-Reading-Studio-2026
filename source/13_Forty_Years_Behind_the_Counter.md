## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> Last month, I interviewed Mrs. Park, who has run a small stationery shop near our school for forty years. I had walked past her shop hundreds of times, but I had never really talked to her. I wanted to know how a tiny shop could survive for so long.
>
> First, I asked her how she had started the business. She told me that she had opened the shop when she was twenty-six. She said that she had borrowed money from her uncle and that she had paid it back in three years. She added that the first year had been the hardest of her life.
>
> Then I asked whether big online stores worried her. She smiled and said that they did. She explained that many of her old customers now bought pens and notebooks online. However, she said that she still had one thing that online stores did not have.
>
> I asked her what that was. "Names," she answered. She told me that she knew the names of most of the students who came in. She said she remembered which pens they liked and which teachers gave them hard homework. Some students, she said, came in just to talk to her after a bad test.
>
> Next, I asked her if she had ever thought about closing the shop. She said that she had thought about it twice. Both times, she explained, a former student had visited her and asked her to stay open. One of them was now a teacher at our school.
>
> At the end of the interview, I asked her what advice she would give to young people who wanted to start a business. She thought for a moment. Then she told me to remember people, not just products. She said that people forget prices, but they never forget how you made them feel.
>
> When I left the shop, I bought a pen that I did not really need. Mrs. Park asked me my name. I told her, and she said she would remember it.

## 2회차 · 문장 단위 직독직해

> 한 문장을 한 호흡에 읽는 연습입니다. 1회차에서 끊었던 지점을 의식하되, 멈추지 않고 이어 읽습니다.

### 문단 1

**① Last month, I interviewed Mrs. Park, who has run a small stationery shop near our school for forty years.**
지난달 나는 우리 학교 근처에서 40년째 작은 문구점을 운영하고 계신 박 씨를 인터뷰했다.
어휘: interview 인터뷰하다 · , who (계속적 용법) · run 운영하다 · stationery 문구

**② I had walked past her shop hundreds of times, but I had never really talked to her.**
그 가게 앞을 수백 번 지나다녔지만, 제대로 이야기를 나눠 본 적은 한 번도 없었다.
어휘: walk past ~옆을 지나가다 · hundreds of 수백의

**③ I wanted to know how a tiny shop could survive for so long.**
그렇게 작은 가게가 어떻게 그토록 오래 버틸 수 있었는지 알고 싶었다.
어휘: tiny 아주 작은 · survive 살아남다

### 문단 2

**④ First, I asked her how she had started the business.**
먼저 어떻게 가게를 시작하셨는지 여쭈었다.
어휘: ask + 의문사 + S + V (간접의문문)

**⑤ She told me that she had opened the shop when she was twenty-six.**
스물여섯 살 때 가게를 여셨다고 했다.
어휘: tell + 사람 + that ~에게 …라고 말하다 · 과거 → had p.p. (시제 일치)

**⑥ She said that she had borrowed money from her uncle and that she had paid it back in three years.**
삼촌에게 돈을 빌려서 3년 만에 다 갚으셨다고 했다.
어휘: borrow 빌리다 · pay back 갚다

**⑦ She added that the first year had been the hardest of her life.**
첫해가 인생에서 가장 힘든 시기였다는 말도 덧붙이셨다.
어휘: add 덧붙이다

그다음 대형 온라인 쇼핑몰이 걱정되지 않으시는지 여쭈었다.
어휘: ask whether ~인지 묻다 · worry 걱정시키다

**⑨ She smiled and said that they did.**
그녀는 웃으며 걱정된다고 하셨다.

**⑩ She explained that many of her old customers now bought pens and notebooks online.**
오랜 손님 중 상당수가 이제는 펜과 공책을 온라인으로 산다고 설명하셨다.

**⑫ I asked her what that was.**

**㉔ She said that people forget prices, but they never forget how you made them feel.**

**㉕ When I left the shop, I bought a pen that I did not really need.**

**㉗ I told her, and she said she would remember it.**

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 직접화법 → 간접화법 바꾸기

| 문장 종류 | 직접화법 (인터뷰 현장의 실제 말) | 간접화법 (기사 속 전달) |
|---|---|---|
| 평서문 | "I **opened** the shop when I **was** twenty-six." | She told me that she **had opened** the shop when she was twenty-six. |
| 의문사 의문문 | "How **did** you **start** the business?" | I asked her **how she had started** the business. |
| Yes/No 의문문 | "**Do** big online stores **worry** you?" | I asked **whether** big online stores **worried** her. |
| Yes/No 의문문 | "**Have** you ever **thought** about closing?" | I asked her **if** she **had** ever **thought** about closing. |
| 명령문 | "**Remember** people, not just products." | She told me **to remember** people, not just products. |
| 미래 | "I **will remember** it." | She said she **would remember** it. |

### 2. 시제 일치의 규칙

전달 동사(said, told, asked)가 과거이면 전달되는 내용의 시제도 한 단계 과거로 옮깁니다.

| 직접화법 | → | 간접화법 |
|---|---|---|
| 현재 (buy, know) | → | 과거 (bought, knew) |
| 과거 (opened) | → | 과거완료 (had opened) |
| 현재완료 (have thought) | → | 과거완료 (had thought) |
| will / can | → | would / could |

**예외.** 변하지 않는 진리나 일반적 사실은 현재시제를 유지할 수 있습니다.

> She said that people **forget** prices, but they never **forget** how you made them feel.

이 문장은 박 씨 개인의 과거 경험이 아니라 늘 참인 일반론이므로 현재시제가 그대로 남았습니다.

### 3. 간접의문문의 어순

의문문이 문장 안으로 들어가면 **의문사 + 주어 + 동사**의 평서문 어순이 됩니다.

- ❌ I asked her how **did she start** the business.
- ✅ I asked her how **she had started** the business.
- ✅ I asked her what **that was**. (← "What is that?")

### 4. say vs tell

| 동사 | 구조 | 예 |
|---|---|---|
| say | say (that) ~ / say to + 사람 | She **said** that she had thought about it twice. |
| tell | tell + 사람 + (that) ~ / tell + 사람 + to부정사 | She **told me** that she knew the names. / She **told me to remember** people. |

tell 뒤에는 듣는 사람이 바로 와야 합니다. said me (❌), told me (✅).
