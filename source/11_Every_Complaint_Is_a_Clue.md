## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> Most people hear complaints every day. A friend who is always late blames the bus. A parent complains about a kitchen drawer that never closes properly. A classmate groans about a textbook which is too heavy to carry. We usually treat these comments as background noise. However, people who build successful businesses often listen to them very differently. To them, every complaint is a clue.
>
> A complaint is a small signal that something is not working. It tells you three useful things. First, it shows a problem that real people have. Second, it reveals the emotion that comes with the problem, such as anger, boredom, or stress. Third, and most importantly, it points to a person whose life would be better if the problem disappeared.
>
> Consider a simple example. A student whose school had no lockers noticed that many students carried heavy bags all day. Everyone complained, but nobody did anything. The student started a small service that let students leave books at a nearby shop for a monthly fee. The idea was not brilliant. It was simply an answer to a complaint which everyone had heard but no one had taken seriously.
>
> Of course, not every complaint leads to a business. Some problems are too rare, and others are things that people do not want to pay to solve. The trick is to look for complaints that are repeated, strong, and shared by many people. A complaint that you hear once is a story. A complaint that you hear every week is a pattern.
>
> You can practice this skill starting today. Carry a small notebook, or use the notes app on your phone. Each time you hear someone complain, write down what they said, who said it, and how strongly they felt. After a month, read through your list. You will probably find a few complaints which appear again and again.
>
> Those repeated complaints are where your search should begin. They will not hand you a finished idea. But they will point you toward the people who need help most, and the people who need help are the people who will become your first customers.

**㉚ They will not hand you a finished idea.**
불평이 완성된 아이디어를 건네주지는 않는다.
어휘: hand 건네주다 · finished 완성된

**㉛ But they will point you toward the people who need help most, and the people who need help are the people who will become your first customers.**
하지만 도움이 가장 필요한 사람들에게로 당신을 이끌어 줄 것이고, 도움이 필요한 그 사람들이 바로 당신의 첫 손님이 될 것이다.
어휘: point A toward B A를 B 쪽으로 이끌다

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 관계대명사 한눈에 보기

관계대명사는 앞의 명사(선행사)를 뒤에서 설명하는 절을 이끕니다. 해석은 "~하는 (명사)"입니다.

| 관계대명사 | 선행사 | 격 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|---|
| who | 사람 | 주격 | a friend **who** is always late |
| which | 사물·동물 | 주격·목적격 | a textbook **which** is too heavy to carry |
| that | 사람·사물 | 주격·목적격 | a kitchen drawer **that** never closes properly |
| whose | 사람·사물 | 소유격 | a person **whose** life would be better |
| what | 선행사 포함 | — | write down **what** they said (= the things that) |

### 2. 주격과 목적격 구별하기

| 구분 | 관계대명사 뒤 | 예 | 생략 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 주격 | 바로 동사 | a drawer **that never closes** | 불가 |
| 목적격 | 주어 + 동사 | a problem **that real people have** | 가능 → a problem real people have |

목적격 관계대명사는 자주 생략됩니다. 09편의 "people **you can reach**", "the words **they used**", 10편의 "the one **you like most**"가 바로 목적격 관계대명사가 생략된 형태입니다.

### 3. 관계대명사 that vs 동격의 that

| 문장 | that 뒤 | 정체 |
|---|---|---|
| a problem **that** real people have | 목적어가 빠진 불완전한 절 | 관계대명사 |
| a small signal **that** something is not working | 빠진 성분이 없는 완전한 절 | 동격 접속사 (= 신호의 내용) |

that 뒤 절이 완전한지 불완전한지가 판별 기준입니다. 이 구별은 수능 어법 문제에 자주 등장합니다.

### 4. 미리보기: 관계부사와 가정법

- Those complaints are **where** your search should begin. → 관계부사(16편)
- a person whose life **would be** better **if** the problem **disappeared** → 가정법 과거(21편)
