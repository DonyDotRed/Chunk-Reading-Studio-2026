## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> A cup of coffee in a city café might cost around five dollars. Many customers assume that most of this money goes to the café owner as profit. A closer look at the price, however, reveals a very different picture. The five dollars must be divided among many people and costs, and the owner's share is often surprisingly small.
>
> The coffee beans themselves account for only a small portion of the price, perhaps around thirty to fifty cents per cup. Milk, cups, lids, and sugar add a little more. These are called the cost of goods, and they rise and fall with the market. A bad harvest in a coffee-growing country can force cafés on the other side of the world to raise their prices.
>
> The largest costs are usually rent and labor. A busy location attracts more customers, but it also demands higher rent. The need for skilled baristas during long opening hours adds a significant amount in wages. In many cafés, these two costs together take up more than half of every dollar earned.
>
> Then there are the costs that customers never see. Electricity keeps the espresso machine hot all day. Regular maintenance prevents expensive breakdowns. Insurance, cleaning, card-payment fees, and taxes all take their share. Even an empty seat has a cost, because the space it occupies is being paid for.
>
> After all these deductions, the owner may keep only a few cents to perhaps fifty cents from each cup as profit. A slight increase in rent or a drop in daily sales can turn that small profit into a loss. This explains why so many small cafés close within their first few years, even when they seem busy. A careful owner therefore watches every small cost, because a few cents saved on each cup can add up to thousands of dollars over a year.
>
> Understanding this structure changes how we see prices. The decision to charge five dollars rather than four is rarely a sign of greed. More often, it reflects the reality of keeping a small business alive. For anyone hoping to start a business, the lesson is clear: the price a customer sees is only the surface. Success depends on knowing what lies beneath it.

### 1. 무생물 주어 구문

영어는 사물·추상 개념을 주어로 삼아 "사물이 사람에게 무언가를 하게 한다"는 식으로 자주 씁니다. 한국어로 직역하면 어색하므로, **주어를 원인·조건·수단의 부사절처럼 풀어** 해석합니다.

| 영어 원문 | 직역 (어색함) | 자연스러운 해석 |
|---|---|---|
| **A closer look** at the price reveals a different picture. | 가격에 대한 더 자세한 관찰이 다른 그림을 드러낸다. | 가격을 **자세히 보면** 다른 그림이 드러난다. |
| **A bad harvest** can force cafés to raise their prices. | 흉작이 카페들이 가격을 올리도록 강요할 수 있다. | 흉작이 **들면** 카페들은 가격을 올릴 수밖에 없다. |
| **A busy location** attracts more customers. | 붐비는 위치가 더 많은 손님을 끌어들인다. | 붐비는 **곳에 있으면** 손님이 더 많이 온다. |
| **Electricity** keeps the machine hot. | 전기가 기계를 뜨겁게 유지한다. | 기계를 뜨겁게 **유지하려면** 전기가 든다. |
| **This** explains why many cafés close. | 이것이 왜 많은 카페가 문을 닫는지 설명한다. | **이 때문에** 많은 카페가 문을 닫는다. |
| **Understanding** this structure changes how we see prices. | 이 구조를 이해하는 것이 우리가 가격을 보는 방식을 바꾼다. | 이 구조를 **이해하면** 가격을 보는 눈이 달라진다. |

### 2. 명사화 (nominalization) 입문

동사나 형용사로 표현할 내용을 명사구로 바꾸면 문장이 압축되고 객관적인 느낌이 납니다. 대학 수준 글쓰기와 학술 논문에서 가장 두드러지는 특징입니다.

| 동사·형용사 중심 (일상적) | 명사 중심 (학술적) |
|---|---|
| Rent **increases slightly**. | **a slight increase in** rent |
| Daily sales **drop**. | **a drop in** daily sales |
| They **need** skilled baristas. | **the need for** skilled baristas |
| They **decide** to charge five dollars. | **the decision to** charge five dollars |
| They **deduct** these costs. | these **deductions** |

**명사화와 무생물 주어는 한 묶음입니다.** 명사화된 덩어리가 그대로 주어가 되면서 무생물 주어 문장이 만들어집니다.

> **A slight increase in rent** can turn that small profit into a loss.
> = **If rent increases slightly**, that small profit can turn into a loss.

### 3. 비율·합계 표현

| 표현 | 의미 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|
| account for | (비율을) 차지하다 | The beans **account for** only a small portion of the price. |
| take up | (공간·비율을) 차지하다 | These two costs **take up** more than half. |
| add up to | 합계가 ~이 되다 | A few cents **can add up to** thousands of dollars. |

**수치에 대한 주의.** 이 글의 금액은 구조를 설명하기 위한 예시입니다. 실제 비율은 지역, 가게 규모, 메뉴에 따라 크게 다르므로 perhaps, around, may 같은 완곡 표현(24편)을 함께 썼습니다.
