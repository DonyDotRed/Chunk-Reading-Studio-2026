## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> When I turned sixteen, my aunt gave me a plain notebook. On the first page, she had written one sentence: "Write down what you notice." I did not understand what she meant, and I wondered whether it was a joke. Still, I decided that I would try it for a month.
>
> At first, I did not know what I should write. The things I noticed seemed too small to matter. I wrote that the school bus was always late on Mondays. I wrote that my little brother hated how his backpack hurt his shoulders. I noted that our neighbor walked her dog at exactly seven every morning.
>
> After a few weeks, I realized that the notebook was changing how I saw the world. I began to ask why things happened. Why was the bus late only on Mondays? Was it true that every backpack hurt? Whether these questions had answers or not, asking them made me more curious.
>
> It was surprising that so many of my notes were about problems. Almost half of the pages described something that annoyed someone. What I had thought was a diary was slowly becoming a list of opportunities. I showed it to my aunt, and she smiled. She said that this was exactly what she had hoped would happen.
>
> One note eventually became a small project. My brother and I designed simple shoulder pads that could be attached to any backpack. We sold them to twenty classmates. The money was small, but the lesson was large. I learned that ideas do not come from nowhere. They come from what we pay attention to.
>
> Today, I still carry a notebook. I do not know whether my next idea will succeed, but I know where it will come from. It will come from a small sentence written on an ordinary day. What I learned from my aunt is simple: if you write down what you notice, you will start to notice what matters.

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 명사절의 종류

명사절은 문장 안에서 주어·목적어·보어 자리에 들어가는 "문장 크기의 명사"입니다.

| 명사절 | 의미 | 자리 | 이 글의 예 |
|---|---|---|---|
| that + 완전한 절 | ~라는 것 | 목적어 | I learned **that ideas do not come from nowhere**. |
| whether / if + 절 | ~인지 아닌지 | 목적어 | I wondered **whether it was a joke**. |
| 의문사 + S + V | 누가/무엇을/왜/어디서 ~하는지 | 목적어 | I did not understand **what she meant**. / I know **where it will come from**. |
| what + 불완전한 절 | ~하는 것 (관계대명사) | 주어·목적어·보어 | **What I learned from my aunt** is simple. |

### 2. 가주어 it ~ 진주어 that

that절이 주어로 쓰이면 문장 앞이 무거워지므로, 주어 자리에 it을 두고 that절은 뒤로 보냅니다.

| 원래 형태 (어색함) | 가주어 구문 (자연스러움) |
|---|---|
| That so many of my notes were about problems was surprising. | **It** was surprising **that** so many of my notes were about problems. |
| That every backpack hurt was true? | Was **it** true **that** every backpack hurt? |

이 it은 "그것"으로 해석하지 않습니다.

### 3. whether vs if

둘 다 "~인지 아닌지"이지만, 쓸 수 있는 자리가 다릅니다.

| 조건 | whether | if |
|---|---|---|
| 목적어 자리 | ✅ I wondered **whether** it was a joke. | ✅ I wondered **if** it was a joke. |
| 문장 맨 앞(주어) | ✅ **Whether** he comes is not important. | ❌ |
| 바로 뒤에 or not | ✅ **whether or not** | ❌ |
| 전치사 뒤 | ✅ about **whether** | ❌ |

"**Whether** these questions had answers **or not**, asking them…"은 명사절이 아니라 "~이든 아니든"이라는 양보 부사절입니다. 23편 양보 구문에서 다시 다룹니다.

### 4. 의문사 what vs 관계대명사 what

| 문장 | 해석 | 정체 |
|---|---|---|
| I did not understand **what she meant**. | 그녀가 **무엇을** 뜻했는지 | 의문사 (무엇) |
| Write down **what you notice**. | 네가 알아차린 **것**을 | 관계대명사 (~하는 것 = the thing that) |

두 해석이 모두 가능한 경우도 많습니다. 문맥상 "궁금함·모름"이 들어 있으면 의문사, "그 대상 자체"를 가리키면 관계대명사로 보면 됩니다.

### 5. 미리보기: 명사절 속 삽입

> This was exactly **what she had hoped would happen**.

원래는 "She had hoped (that) **it** would happen."이고, it 자리를 what이 받아 앞으로 나간 구조입니다. "그녀가 일어나기를 바랐던 것"으로 해석합니다.
