## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> This case study examines Morning Loaf, a small neighborhood bakery that grew from twelve loaves on its opening day to more than five hundred loaves per morning over approximately twelve years. The case was selected because it illustrates, at a modest scale, a practice that management scholars have described as co-creation: the involvement of customers in shaping the products and experiences offered to them. The study draws on the bakery's publicly displayed customer request board and on informal conversations with its owner.
>
> **Background**
>
> The bakery opened in a building that had been unoccupied for approximately five years, in a location that local observers considered commercially unpromising. The founder, Mrs. Han, had limited capital. Equipment was purchased secondhand from a closing restaurant, furniture was improvised from used materials, and signage was produced by a local student. Sales on the first day amounted to twelve loaves.
>
> **Practices**
>
> The distinctive feature of the case is a single, low-cost practice introduced shortly after opening. Customers were invited to answer the question "What kind of bread do you miss?" on a large board near the entrance. Requests were reviewed weekly; selected recipes were developed and offered as free samples, and customers were asked for candid feedback. Products that received positive responses were added to the menu, while those that did not were withdrawn without announcement. Notably, the board has remained in use throughout the bakery's history rather than being abandoned once the business became established. The board also served as an informal record of demand, since repeated requests for the same product indicated its likely popularity.
>
> **Outcomes**
>
> Over twelve years, daily production increased to more than five hundred loaves, the workforce grew to eight employees, three of whom had previously been regular customers, and the bakery was recognized as the best small business in its district. While these outcomes cannot be attributed to the request board alone, the owner consistently identifies it as the central element of the bakery's development.
>
> **Analysis**
>
> The case is consistent with several mechanisms proposed in the literature on co-creation. First, customer input reduced uncertainty about demand: new products were tested on precisely the people who had requested them. Second, participation appears to have strengthened customers' sense of ownership, which may help to explain both repeat visits and the recruitment of employees from the customer base. Third, the public nature of the board signaled responsiveness, potentially attracting customers who had not themselves contributed requests.
>
> **Transferability**
>
> Several features of the case may limit its applicability elsewhere. The bakery operated in a stable residential community, where repeat customers were numerous and personal relationships were feasible. Bread is also a low-cost product that can be adapted quickly, so the cost of experimenting with new recipes was small. Businesses with expensive products, long development cycles, or transient customers may find the same practice considerably harder to implement. Moreover, the owner was personally present in the shop for most of its history, which allowed requests to be discussed face to face; a larger chain might struggle to maintain the same personal connection.
>
> **Conclusion**
>
> The Morning Loaf case suggests that co-creation does not require sophisticated technology or large budgets. A visible, persistent channel for customer requests, combined with a disciplined process for testing and selecting them, allowed a resource-poor business to align its products closely with local demand. As with any single case, however, these conclusions should be regarded as illustrative rather than general.

### 문단 1

**① This case study examines Morning Loaf, a small neighborhood bakery that grew from twelve loaves on its opening day to more than five hundred loaves per morning over approximately twelve years.**
이 사례 연구는 개업 첫날 빵 12덩이에서 약 12년 만에 아침마다 500덩이 넘게 굽는 곳으로 성장한 작은 동네 빵집, 모닝 로프를 살펴본다.
어휘: case study 사례 연구 · examine 검토하다 · loaf–loaves 빵 덩이

**② The case was selected because it illustrates, at a modest scale, a practice that management scholars have described as co-creation: the involvement of customers in shaping the products and experiences offered to them.**
이 사례를 고른 이유는 경영학자들이 공동 창조, 즉 고객이 자신에게 제공되는 상품과 경험을 만드는 데 참여하는 관행이라고 부르는 것을 작은 규모로 잘 보여 주기 때문이다.
어휘: select 선정하다 · illustrate 예증하다 · modest 소박한, 작은 · practice 관행, 실천 · management 경영 · scholar 학자 · co-creation 공동 창조 · involvement 참여, 관여 · shape 형성하다

**③ The study draws on the bakery's publicly displayed customer request board and on informal conversations with its owner.**
이 연구는 빵집에 공개적으로 걸린 고객 요청 게시판과 주인과의 비공식 대화를 자료로 삼았다.
어휘: draw on ~을 활용하다 · publicly 공개적으로 · display 게시하다 · request 요청 · informal 비공식적인

### 문단 2 · Background

**④ The bakery opened in a building that had been unoccupied for approximately five years, in a location that local observers considered commercially unpromising.**
빵집은 약 5년간 비어 있던 건물, 지역 사람들이 장사가 안 될 곳이라고 여기던 자리에서 문을 열었다.
어휘: unoccupied 비어 있는 · observer 관찰자 · consider A (to be) B A를 B로 여기다 · commercially 상업적으로

**⑤ The founder, Mrs. Han, had limited capital.**
창업자인 한 씨는 자본이 넉넉하지 않았다.
어휘: founder 창업자 · capital 자본

**⑥ Equipment was purchased secondhand from a closing restaurant, furniture was improvised from used materials, and signage was produced by a local student.**
장비는 폐업하는 식당에서 중고로 샀고, 가구는 중고 자재로 만들었으며, 간판은 동네 학생이 제작했다.
어휘: equipment 장비 · purchase 구입하다 · secondhand 중고로 · furniture 가구 · improvise 임시변통하다 · material 자재 · signage 간판류

**⑦ Sales on the first day amounted to twelve loaves.**
첫날 판매량은 12덩이였다.
어휘: amount to (합계가) ~에 달하다

### 문단 3 · Practices

**⑧ The distinctive feature of the case is a single, low-cost practice introduced shortly after opening.**
이 사례의 독특한 점은 개업 직후 도입한 단 하나의 저비용 관행이다.
어휘: distinctive 독특한 · feature 특징 · low-cost 저비용의 · introduce 도입하다 · shortly after ~직후에

**⑨ Customers were invited to answer the question "What kind of bread do you miss?" on a large board near the entrance.**
고객들은 입구 근처의 큰 게시판에 "어떤 빵이 그리우세요?"라는 질문에 답해 달라는 요청을 받았다.
어휘: invite 청하다 · entrance 입구

**⑩ Requests were reviewed weekly; selected recipes were developed and offered as free samples, and customers were asked for candid feedback.**
요청은 매주 검토했다. 그중 고른 조리법을 개발해 무료 시식으로 내놓고, 고객에게 솔직한 의견을 구했다.
어휘: review 검토하다 · weekly 매주 · recipe 조리법 · develop 개발하다 · sample 시식품, 견본 · candid 솔직한

**⑪ Products that received positive responses were added to the menu, while those that did not were withdrawn without announcement.**
반응이 좋은 상품은 메뉴에 올렸고, 그렇지 않은 상품은 따로 알리지 않고 뺐다.
어휘: response 반응 · those (= products) 대용 · did not (receive positive responses) 생략 · withdraw 철회하다, 빼다 · announcement 공지

**⑫ Notably, the board has remained in use throughout the bakery's history rather than being abandoned once the business became established.**
주목할 점은, 가게가 자리를 잡은 뒤에도 게시판을 없애지 않고 지금까지 계속 써 왔다는 것이다.
어휘: notably 특히, 주목할 만하게 · remain in use 계속 사용되다 · throughout ~내내 · abandon 버리다 · established 자리 잡은

**⑬ The board also served as an informal record of demand, since repeated requests for the same product indicated its likely popularity.**
같은 상품을 원하는 요청이 반복되면 그 상품이 인기를 끌 가능성이 높다는 뜻이었으므로, 게시판은 수요를 보여 주는 비공식 기록 역할도 했다.
어휘: serve as ~의 역할을 하다 · record 기록 · indicate 나타내다 · popularity 인기

### 문단 4 · Outcomes

**⑭ Over twelve years, daily production increased to more than five hundred loaves, the workforce grew to eight employees, three of whom had previously been regular customers, and the bakery was recognized as the best small business in its district.**
12년 동안 하루 생산량은 500덩이 이상으로 늘었고, 직원은 8명이 되었는데 그중 3명은 원래 단골손님이었다. 빵집은 구에서 가장 우수한 소규모 사업체로 인정받았다.
어휘: production 생산량 · workforce 인력 · employee 직원 · of whom (관계대명사) · previously 이전에 · recognize 인정하다 · district 구, 지역

**⑮ While these outcomes cannot be attributed to the request board alone, the owner consistently identifies it as the central element of the bakery's development.**
이 결과를 요청 게시판 하나의 덕으로만 돌릴 수는 없지만, 주인은 일관되게 게시판을 빵집 발전의 핵심 요소로 꼽는다.
어휘: while (양보) · attribute A to B A를 B 탓/덕으로 돌리다 · consistently 일관되게 · identify 지목하다 · central 핵심의 · element 요소 · development 발전

### 문단 5 · Analysis

**⑯ The case is consistent with several mechanisms proposed in the literature on co-creation.**
이 사례는 공동 창조에 관한 문헌에서 제시된 여러 메커니즘과 부합한다.
어휘: be consistent with ~와 일치하다 · mechanism 메커니즘 · literature 문헌 · propose 제시하다

**⑰ First, customer input reduced uncertainty about demand: new products were tested on precisely the people who had requested them.**
첫째, 고객의 의견이 수요에 관한 불확실성을 줄였다. 새 상품을 바로 그것을 요청한 사람들에게 시험했기 때문이다.
어휘: input 의견, 투입 · uncertainty 불확실성 · demand 수요 · precisely 바로, 정확히

**⑱ Second, participation appears to have strengthened customers' sense of ownership, which may help to explain both repeat visits and the recruitment of employees from the customer base.**
둘째, 참여가 고객의 주인 의식을 키운 것으로 보이며, 이는 재방문과 고객층에서 직원을 채용한 일을 함께 설명하는 데 도움이 될 수 있다.
어휘: participation 참여 · strengthen 강화하다 · ownership 소유, 주인 의식 · repeat visit 재방문 · recruitment 채용 · customer base 고객층

**⑲ Third, the public nature of the board signaled responsiveness, potentially attracting customers who had not themselves contributed requests.**
셋째, 게시판이 공개되어 있다는 점이 가게가 고객의 말을 듣는다는 신호가 되어, 직접 요청을 내지 않은 고객까지 끌어들였을 가능성이 있다.
어휘: nature 성격, 본질 · signal 신호를 보내다 · responsiveness 응답성 · potentially 잠재적으로 · contribute 기여하다, 내다

### 문단 6 · Transferability

**⑳ Several features of the case may limit its applicability elsewhere.**
이 사례의 몇 가지 특징은 다른 곳에 적용하는 데 한계가 될 수 있다.
어휘: applicability 적용 가능성

**㉑ The bakery operated in a stable residential community, where repeat customers were numerous and personal relationships were feasible.**
빵집은 재방문 고객이 많고 개인적 관계를 맺기 쉬운 안정적인 주택가에서 운영되었다.
어휘: operate 운영되다 · stable 안정적인 · residential 주거의 · community 공동체 · numerous 많은 · feasible 실현 가능한

**㉒ Bread is also a low-cost product that can be adapted quickly, so the cost of experimenting with new recipes was small.**
또한 빵은 값싸고 빨리 바꿀 수 있는 상품이어서 새 조리법을 실험하는 비용이 작았다.
어휘: adapt 바꾸다, 조정하다 · experiment 실험하다

**㉓ Businesses with expensive products, long development cycles, or transient customers may find the same practice considerably harder to implement.**
상품이 비싸거나, 개발 주기가 길거나, 고객이 한 번 스쳐 가는 사업체는 같은 관행을 실행하기가 훨씬 어려울 수 있다.
어휘: cycle 주기 · transient 일시적인, 스쳐 가는 · considerably 상당히 · implement 실행하다

**㉔ Moreover, the owner was personally present in the shop for most of its history, which allowed requests to be discussed face to face; a larger chain might struggle to maintain the same personal connection.**
게다가 주인이 가게 역사의 대부분 동안 직접 매장에 있었기 때문에 요청을 얼굴을 맞대고 이야기할 수 있었다. 더 큰 체인점이라면 같은 개인적 유대를 유지하기 어려울 수 있다.
어휘: present 있는, 출석한 · , which (앞 내용 전체) · face to face 대면하여 · chain 체인점 · struggle 애쓰다, 고전하다 · maintain 유지하다 · connection 유대, 연결

### 문단 7 · Conclusion

**㉕ The Morning Loaf case suggests that co-creation does not require sophisticated technology or large budgets.**
모닝 로프 사례는 공동 창조에 정교한 기술이나 큰 예산이 필요하지 않음을 시사한다.
어휘: sophisticated 정교한 · budget 예산

**㉖ A visible, persistent channel for customer requests, combined with a disciplined process for testing and selecting them, allowed a resource-poor business to align its products closely with local demand.**
눈에 잘 띄고 오래 유지되는 고객 요청 통로와, 그 요청을 시험하고 선별하는 규율 있는 과정이 결합되어, 자원이 부족한 가게가 상품을 지역 수요에 긴밀히 맞출 수 있었다.
어휘: visible 눈에 보이는 · persistent 지속적인 · channel 통로, 경로 · disciplined 규율 있는 · process 과정 · resource-poor 자원이 부족한 · align A with B A를 B에 맞추다

**㉗ As with any single case, however, these conclusions should be regarded as illustrative rather than general.**
그러나 단일 사례가 다 그렇듯, 이 결론은 일반화된 것이 아니라 예시적인 것으로 보아야 한다.
어휘: regard A as B A를 B로 여기다 · illustrative 예시적인

### 2. 인과를 조심스럽게 말하는 표현

사례 연구는 "A 다음에 B가 일어났다"는 것은 보여 줄 수 있어도 "A 때문에 B가 일어났다"는 것을 증명하기는 어렵습니다. 그래서 인과 표현을 매우 조심스럽게 씁니다.

| 강한 표현 (피해야 함) | 신중한 표현 (이 글) |
|---|---|
| The board caused the bakery's success. | these outcomes **cannot be attributed to** the request board **alone** |
| Participation strengthened ownership. | participation **appears to have strengthened** customers' sense of ownership |
| This explains repeat visits. | which **may help to explain** both repeat visits and… |
| The board attracted new customers. | **potentially** attracting customers who had not themselves contributed requests |
| The case proves co-creation works. | The case **is consistent with** several mechanisms proposed in the literature |

### 3. 전이 가능성(transferability) 논의
