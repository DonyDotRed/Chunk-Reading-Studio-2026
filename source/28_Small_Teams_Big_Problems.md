## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> In the spring, three students at our school formed a team to build a simple study-planner app. Jiwon handled the design, Hyun wrote the code, and Seo-yeon talked to potential users. On paper, the team looked perfect. Each member had a clear role, and each was skilled in it. Yet within two months, the project had nearly collapsed. This case analysis examines why.
>
> The first problem was communication. Jiwon assumed that Hyun understood her designs, and Hyun assumed that he did. Neither checked. As a result, the first version of the app looked very different from the one Jiwon had drawn. When she pointed this out, Hyun said he had done what he thought she wanted. She said he hadn't. Both were partly right.
>
> The second problem was priorities. Seo-yeon had interviewed twenty students, and most of them wanted a simple reminder function. Jiwon, however, wanted to add colorful themes, and so did Hyun. Seo-yeon argued that the users' needs were more important than those of the team. The others agreed in principle but continued to work on the themes anyway, because doing so was more fun.
>
> The third problem was time. All three had exams in May. Hyun believed the team should pause the project, but Jiwon did not want to. "If we stop now, we'll never restart," she said. "I hope not," Hyun replied, "but I think we should." In the end, they stopped for three weeks. Fortunately, they did restart, though with less energy than before.
>
> What finally saved the project was a simple change. The team began meeting for fifteen minutes every Monday. Each member answered three questions: What did I do last week? What will I do this week? What is blocking me? The meetings were short, but they made hidden problems visible early. Within a month, the reminder function was finished, and the themes were postponed.
>
> Several lessons can be drawn from this case. Small teams are often assumed to communicate easily, but this case suggests otherwise. When everyone is busy, small misunderstandings grow quietly. A regular meeting is not a sign of distrust; it is a tool for catching problems while they are still small. Similarly, user needs should come before team preferences, even when the latter are more exciting. The app is now used by over two hundred students. Its success came not from talent alone, but from learning to talk to each other, and to the users, regularly.

**㉖ What finally saved the project was a simple change.**
프로젝트를 결국 살린 것은 간단한 변화였다.
어휘: What ~ was … (강조)

**㉗ The team began meeting for fifteen minutes every Monday.**
팀은 매주 월요일 15분씩 회의를 하기 시작했다.

**㉘ Each member answered three questions: What did I do last week?**
각자 세 가지 질문에 답했다. 지난주에 나는 무엇을 했나?

**㉙ What will I do this week?**
이번 주에는 무엇을 할 것인가?

**㉚ What is blocking me?**
무엇이 나를 가로막고 있나?
어휘: block 막다

**㉛ The meetings were short, but they made hidden problems visible early.**
회의는 짧았지만, 숨은 문제를 일찍 드러내 주었다.
어휘: hidden 숨겨진 · visible 보이는

**㉜ Within a month, the reminder function was finished, and the themes were postponed.**
한 달 만에 알림 기능이 완성되었고, 테마는 뒤로 미뤄졌다.
어휘: postpone 미루다

### 문단 6

**㉝ Several lessons can be drawn from this case.**
이 사례에서 몇 가지 교훈을 얻을 수 있다.
어휘: draw a lesson 교훈을 얻다

**㉞ Small teams are often assumed to communicate easily, but this case suggests otherwise.**
작은 팀은 흔히 소통이 쉽다고 여겨지지만, 이 사례는 그렇지 않음을 보여 준다.
어휘: otherwise 그렇지 않게 (대용)

**㉟ When everyone is busy, small misunderstandings grow quietly.**
모두가 바쁘면 작은 오해가 조용히 커진다.
어휘: misunderstanding 오해

**㊱ A regular meeting is not a sign of distrust; it is a tool for catching problems while they are still small.**
정기 회의는 불신의 표시가 아니라, 문제가 아직 작을 때 잡아내는 도구다.
어휘: distrust 불신

**㊲ Similarly, user needs should come before team preferences, even when the latter are more exciting.**
마찬가지로, 팀의 선호가 더 신나 보이더라도 사용자의 요구가 먼저여야 한다.
어휘: preference 선호 · the latter 후자 (대용)

**㊳ The app is now used by over two hundred students.**
그 앱은 지금 200명이 넘는 학생이 쓰고 있다.

**㊴ Its success came not from talent alone, but from learning to talk to each other, and to the users, regularly.**
그 성공은 재능만이 아니라, 서로에게 그리고 사용자에게 꾸준히 말을 거는 법을 배운 데서 나왔다.
어휘: not A but B · (talk 생략)

## 문법 포인트

### 1. 대용: 앞에 나온 말을 짧게 받기

영어는 같은 말의 반복을 매우 싫어합니다. 그래서 앞의 내용을 짧은 말로 대신 받는 장치가 발달했습니다. 독해할 때는 **대용어가 무엇을 받는지 찾는 것**이 핵심입니다.

| 대용어 | 대신하는 것 | 이 글의 예 | 원래 내용 |
|---|---|---|---|
| do / did | 동사구 | Hyun assumed that he **did**. | understood her designs |
| so + 조동사 + 주어 | "~도 그렇다" | **so did** Hyun | Hyun wanted to add colorful themes, too |
| neither | 부정의 "둘 다 아님" | **Neither** checked. | Jiwon didn't check, and Hyun didn't check |
| one / ones | 가산명사 | different from **the one** Jiwon had drawn | the version |
| that / those of | 앞의 명사 + of | more important than **those of** the team | the needs of the team |
| doing so | 앞의 행동 전체 | because **doing so** was more fun | working on the themes |
| so / not | that절 | I hope **not**. | I hope that (we never restart) won't happen |
| the latter / the former | 후자 / 전자 | even when **the latter** are more exciting | team preferences |
| otherwise | 반대의 내용 | this case suggests **otherwise** | that small teams do not communicate easily |
| each | each + 명사 | **each** was skilled in it | each member |

### 2. 생략: 말 자체를 빼기

| 생략 위치 | 이 글의 예 | 생략된 말 |
|---|---|---|
| 대부정사 to 뒤 | Jiwon did not want **to**. | pause the project |
| 조동사 뒤 | I think we **should**. | stop now |
| 완료 조동사 뒤 | She said he **hadn't**. | done what she wanted |
| 의문사 뒤 | This case analysis examines **why**. | the project had nearly collapsed |
| 접속사 뒤 | **though** with less energy than before | though (it was) with less energy |
| 반복되는 동사 | to talk to each other, and **to the users** | (to talk) to the users |

### 3. 강조의 did와 대동사 did 구별

| 문장 | did의 정체 | 해석 |
|---|---|---|
| Hyun assumed that he **did**. | 대동사 (= understood her designs) | 현이도 그렇다고 생각했다 |
| They **did restart**. | 강조 (뒤에 동사원형) | 정말로 다시 시작했다 |

did 바로 뒤에 동사원형이 있으면 강조(22편), 없으면 대동사입니다.

### 4. so do I / neither do I

| 앞 문장 | 맞장구 | 의미 |
|---|---|---|
| Jiwon wanted colorful themes. | **So did** Hyun. | 현이도 원했다 |
| Jiwon didn't check. | **Neither did** Hyun. | 현이도 안 했다 |

긍정에는 so, 부정에는 neither를 쓰고, 그 뒤는 **조동사 + 주어**로 도치됩니다. 조동사는 앞 문장의 시제와 동사에 맞춥니다.
