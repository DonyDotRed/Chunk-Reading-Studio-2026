## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> Ten years ago, I spent forty dollars on a lemonade stand and earned back only nine. At the time, it felt like the end of my career as a businessperson. Looking back, it was actually the beginning of my education. Since then, I have started several small projects, failed at most of them, and learned something from each. This essay brings those lessons together. None of them is new or secret. Taken together, however, they have changed the way I think about work, risk, and people.
>
> To begin with, I now believe that problems are more valuable than ideas. When I was younger, I collected ideas the way some people collect stamps. Very few of them led anywhere, because they were not connected to anyone's real needs. By contrast, the projects that succeeded all started with a complaint I had heard more than once. A good idea, in other words, is usually just a well-understood problem.
>
> Closely related to this is the importance of testing early. Every plan I made on paper looked better than it turned out in reality. The sooner I showed something to real people, the sooner I discovered what was wrong with it. Such discoveries were rarely pleasant, but they were always cheaper than discovering the same things later.
>
> More importantly, I have learned that failure says less about a person than about an approach. For years, I treated each setback as evidence that I was not good enough. That interpretation was not only painful but also inaccurate. When I began to describe failures as experiments that had produced unexpected data, I became calmer and, as a result, more willing to try again.
>
> Another lesson concerns other people. Almost nothing I achieved was achieved alone. Customers corrected my assumptions, partners noticed what I had missed, and mentors asked questions I had been avoiding. The few times I tried to hide a problem from the people around me, it grew larger. The many times I shared it, it grew smaller.
>
> Finally, I have come to see trust as the foundation of everything else. Ideas can be copied, prices can be matched, and products can be improved by competitors. A reputation for honesty, on the other hand, can only be earned slowly. It is, therefore, the one advantage that cannot be quickly taken away.
>
> In short, what I know now is not complicated. Look for problems rather than ideas. Test them before you trust them. Treat failure as information. Ask for help sooner than feels comfortable. Above all, keep your promises. If I had known these things at fifteen, I might have kept my forty dollars. Then again, I might never have learned them at all.

어휘: setback 좌절, 차질

**⑲ That interpretation was not only painful but also inaccurate.**
그런 해석은 고통스러웠을 뿐 아니라 정확하지도 않았다.
어휘: that (앞 문장 지시) · interpretation 해석 · inaccurate 부정확한

**⑳ When I began to describe failures as experiments that had produced unexpected data, I became calmer and, as a result, more willing to try again.**
실패를 예상치 못한 데이터를 낸 실험으로 표현하기 시작하자, 나는 더 차분해졌고 그 덕분에 다시 시도할 마음도 더 커졌다.
어휘: calm 차분한

### 문단 5

**㉑ Another lesson concerns other people.**
또 다른 교훈은 다른 사람들에 관한 것이다.
어휘: another (추가 표지) · concern ~에 관한 것이다

**㉒ Almost nothing I achieved was achieved alone.**
내가 이룬 것 중 혼자 이룬 것은 거의 없다.
어휘: achieve 이루다

**㉓ Customers corrected my assumptions, partners noticed what I had missed, and mentors asked questions I had been avoiding.**
손님들은 내 추측을 바로잡아 주었고, 동료들은 내가 놓친 것을 알아챘으며, 멘토들은 내가 피해 온 질문을 던졌다.
어휘: correct 바로잡다 · assumption 가정, 추측 · mentor 멘토

**㉔ The few times I tried to hide a problem from the people around me, it grew larger.**
주변 사람들에게 문제를 숨기려 했던 몇 번의 경우, 문제는 더 커졌다.

**㉕ The many times I shared it, it grew smaller.**
문제를 나눴던 수많은 경우에는 문제가 더 작아졌다.

### 문단 6

**㉖ Finally, I have come to see trust as the foundation of everything else.**
마지막으로, 나는 신뢰를 다른 모든 것의 토대로 보게 되었다.
어휘: come to ~하게 되다 · foundation 토대

**㉗ Ideas can be copied, prices can be matched, and products can be improved by competitors.**
아이디어는 베낄 수 있고, 가격은 맞출 수 있으며, 상품은 경쟁자가 더 낫게 만들 수 있다.
어휘: match 맞추다 · competitor 경쟁자

**㉘ A reputation for honesty, on the other hand, can only be earned slowly.**
반면 정직하다는 평판은 천천히 얻을 수밖에 없다.
어휘: reputation 평판 · honesty 정직

**㉙ It is, therefore, the one advantage that cannot be quickly taken away.**
그래서 신뢰는 쉽게 빼앗길 수 없는 유일한 강점이다.
어휘: advantage 이점 · take away 빼앗다

### 문단 7

**㉚ In short, what I know now is not complicated.**
요컨대, 지금 내가 아는 것은 복잡하지 않다.
어휘: in short 요컨대 (요약 표지) · complicated 복잡한

**㉛ Look for problems rather than ideas.**
아이디어보다 문제를 찾아라.

**㉜ Test them before you trust them.**
믿기 전에 시험하라.

**㉝ Treat failure as information.**
실패를 정보로 여겨라.

**㉞ Ask for help sooner than feels comfortable.**
마음이 편한 시점보다 조금 더 일찍 도움을 청하라.
어휘: comfortable 편안한

**㉟ Above all, keep your promises.**
무엇보다, 약속을 지켜라.
어휘: above all 무엇보다도

**㊱ If I had known these things at fifteen, I might have kept my forty dollars.**
열다섯 살에 이런 것들을 알았더라면, 그 40달러를 지켰을지도 모른다.
어휘: If + had p.p. (가정법 과거완료) · might have p.p.

**㊲ Then again, I might never have learned them at all.**
하지만 달리 생각해 보면, 그랬다면 이 교훈들을 아예 배우지 못했을지도 모른다.
어휘: then again 하지만 달리 보면

### 1. 담화 응집 장치란

글이 문장들의 단순한 나열이 아니라 하나의 흐름으로 읽히게 만드는 장치입니다. 대학 수준 읽기·쓰기에서 가장 중요한 능력이 바로 이 장치를 알아보고 직접 쓰는 것입니다. 이 마지막 편은 지금까지 배운 장치를 한 글에 모두 담았습니다.

### 4. 처음과 끝의 연결

이 글의 첫 문장과 마지막 문장은 01편 "The Lemonade Stand That Lost Money"로 돌아갑니다. 01편의 화자가 쓴 40달러와 번 9달러가 30편에서 같은 숫자로 다시 등장합니다. 01편은 과거시제 한 가지로 쓴 글이었고, 30편은 그동안 배운 거의 모든 구문으로 같은 경험을 다시 해석합니다. 두 편을 연달아 낭독하면 문장이 얼마나 복잡해졌는지, 그리고 그 문장을 이제 얼마나 편하게 읽는지 체감할 수 있습니다.
